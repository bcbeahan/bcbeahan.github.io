phd

m6A dysregulation in breast cancer, studied with Oxford Nanopore Direct RNA sequencing

I am a bioinformatics PhD student at the Institut Jules Bordet in Brussels, enrolled jointly at the Vrije Universiteit Brussel and the Université libre de Bruxelles. I started in March 2024.

I am supervised by Dr. Jana Jeschke (ULB) and Dr. Catharina Olsen (VUB).

Supervisors

  • Dr. Jana Jeschke (ULB) leads the Applied Cancer Epigenomics and Epitranscriptomics group at the Institut Jules Bordet, on the ULB Erasme campus. Her group studies DNA and RNA modifications in clinical cancer samples, with a focus on m6A in breast cancer. She holds an ERC Starting Grant, EpiResist, to build the first single-cell maps of m6A in breast cancer and understand its role in drug resistance.
  • Dr. Catharina Olsen (VUB) is based at UZ Brussel and is a director of the Interuniversity Institute of Bioinformatics in Brussels, (IB)², a joint ULB–VUB bioinformatics institute. Her research focuses on developing and applying machine learning methods for biomedical problems.

Research question

My project investigates the impact of m6A dysregulation in breast cancer. N6-methyladenosine (m6A) is the most common internal modification on messenger RNA, and it helps regulate how transcripts are processed, exported, translated and degraded. The lab’s wider aim is to profile m6A systematically in breast cancer biopsies, so that RNA modifications can add a new layer to patient stratification, help predict outcome and treatment response, and point to new therapeutic targets.

Approach

I apply Oxford Nanopore Direct RNA sequencing to clinical samples. Because it sequences native RNA molecules without converting them to cDNA first, it can detect modifications such as m6A directly, on individual full-length transcripts.
