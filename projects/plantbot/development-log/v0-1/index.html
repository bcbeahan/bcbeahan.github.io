v0.1PlanningProcurementLearning

PlantBot is still at the beginning: I have a plan, an initial hardware order, and a lot to learn. This entry records that starting point as an engineering notebook—not as a finished-product announcement.

Why PlantBot?

I wanted a project that would force me to work across several layers of engineering instead of staying entirely in software. PlantBot gives me a physical system where I need to think about electronics, sensors, embedded programming, data collection, communications, control logic, power, physical actuation, and eventually automation.

Plant care is a useful test system because software decisions ultimately have observable physical consequences. That makes it a grounded place to learn, without pretending the project needs to be bigger than it is.

Design philosophy

The first version is deliberately small: approximately one plant, one soil-moisture sensor, one ESP32, one pump, simple wiring, basic sensor logging, and eventually simple on/off pump control. The guiding principle is to learn each layer individually before adding complexity.

For now I am avoiding many sensors, multiple plants, complicated networking, machine learning, large dashboards, and sophisticated control algorithms. The chain I want to understand is:

Physical measurement→Embedded system→Data→Software decision→Physical actuation

Learning the Stack

Reading sequenceMake: Electronics → Botany for Gardeners → Arduino Cookbook → Making Embedded Systems

01Electronics foundation
NextReading order 1

Make: Electronics, 3rd Edition

Charles Platt

Why I chose it: To understand what I am actually wiring rather than merely copying circuit diagrams.

PlantBot relevance: Breadboards, voltage/current, resistors, diodes, transistors, motors, and basic circuits.

02Plant biology
PlannedReading order 2

Botany for Gardeners, 4th Edition

Brian Capon

Why I chose it: PlantBot should not just be an electronics project; understanding the biological system being monitored matters too.

PlantBot relevance: Roots, water transport, photosynthesis, growth, and plant structure.

03Practical reference
Later referenceReading order 3

Arduino Cookbook, 3rd Edition

Michael Margolis, Brian Jepson & Nicholas Robert Weldin

Why I chose it: A practical reference rather than necessarily a linear read.

PlantBot relevance: Sensors, motors, ADC, communications, networking, and timing. Although PlantBot uses an ESP32, much of the interface material is conceptually useful.

04System design
Later referenceReading order 4

Making Embedded Systems, 2nd Edition

Elecia White

Why I chose it: More advanced reading for after I already have a basic prototype running.

PlantBot relevance: Embedded architecture, debugging, interfaces, sensors, motors, resource constraints, and IoT/system design.
